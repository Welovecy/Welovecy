Cyprus runs on buses. There's no railway and no metro, but there are three kinds of bus service: **urban** buses within towns, **interurban** buses between the major towns, and **rural** buses out to the villages.

## Who runs what

Different companies run different areas, and each has its own fares. The ones you're most likely to use:

- **Cyprus Public Transport:** town buses in Larnaca and Nicosia, including the Larnaca airport routes.
- **Pafos Buses:** Paphos town, the coast and Paphos Airport, including routes **612** and **615**.
- **OSEA:** the Famagusta district, so Ayia Napa, Protaras and Paralimni.
- **Intercity Buses:** the long-distance routes between the major towns.

Always check the operator for each leg of your trip. A ticket from one won't usually work on another.

## Paying for your ticket

**Cash on board** works with the main operators. Cyprus Public Transport publishes its cash fares, and **OSEA** sells paper tickets on the bus **for cash only**, so carry coins and small notes in Ayia Napa and Protaras.

**Motion cards** are Cyprus Public Transport's travel cards. There are paper and plastic versions, and plastic cards can be **charged with period fares**, which makes sense if you're using the buses every day.

**Multi-day tickets:** OSEA lists **1-day, 5-day and 7-day** tickets.

Fares change, so check the operator's own fares page for your dates.

## Making it easy

- **Check the return trip** before you set off, and especially the last bus back.
- **Take a photo of the stop names** for both directions. It saves guessing on the way home.
- **Timetables change** between summer and winter. Use the one for your actual travel date.
- **Validate your ticket** where you're asked to.
- **Need step-free access?** Check the vehicle ramp and the stops with the operator before you travel.

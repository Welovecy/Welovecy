Cape Greco is the wild corner between Ayia Napa and Protaras: sea cliffs, caves cut into the rock and water so clear it looks unreal.

## The park

**Cape Greco National Forest Park** lies **east of Ayia Napa** and covers **385 hectares**. It's a **Natura 2000** site, protected for its plants and wildlife, so stay on the paths.

## The sea caves: please read this

The sea caves are cut into the coastal cliffs, and for years people jumped from them. On **22 May 2026** the **Department of Forests** announced **temporary fencing** at the caves, after serious accidents and **signs of instability in the rock**.

- **Respect the barriers.**
- **Don't jump.**
- Check the signs on the day: which parts are open may change.

## The coastal trail

The official hiking guide lists a **4 km linear** trail towards **Agioi Anargyroi church**, the little chapel on the cliffs, which you can also reach from the main road.

- It's **linear**, so plan for the walk back, or a lift.
- There's **no drinking water** on the route. Carry plenty.
- Wear proper shoes: the rock is sharp and uneven.
- Only walk paths that are signed as open.

Our tip: a shorter out-and-back gives you more flexibility than committing to the whole route.

## The Environmental Centre

The **Cape Greco Environmental Centre** explains the park's nature. It lists adult entry at **€2**, and opens:

- **1 May to 31 October:** every day, **10:00 to 18:30**
- **1 November to 30 April:** Monday to Friday, **10:00 to 14:30**

It closes on public holidays.

## When to go

Early morning or late afternoon. There's almost no shade, and the cliffs get very hot in summer. In winter, it's one of the best walks on the island.

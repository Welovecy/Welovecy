Protaras is a beach resort first. But between swims there's a lovely walk, a couple of chapels with stories, and a trail most visitors never find.

## Walk the promenade

The seafront promenade stretches **more than 4 km**, linking the **Agios Nikolaos fishing shelter** with **Fig Tree Bay**, and has rest areas along the way. You don't have to do all of it. Start at your hotel's end, pick a café or a bay as your turnaround, and walk back as the sun goes down.

## Profitis Elias, the chapel on the hill

The chapel of the **Prophet Elias** sits on a rocky hill above the resort, and the view from the top takes in the whole coast. Go early or late, when it's cooler. [Profitis Elias](/places/profitis-ilias)

## The blue and white chapel at Louma

In Pernera, near **Louma** beach, the small **chapel of Agios Nikolaos** is painted blue and white. It's a quiet stop on a walk along the Pernera coast. [Pernera](/destinations/protaras/pernera)

## Agioi Saranta and the nature trail

The chapel of **Agioi Saranta** (the Forty Saints) is also on the official Protaras map, and the municipality describes the **Ayii Saranda Nature Trail** around it. Wear proper shoes.

## Fig Tree Bay and Konnos

And of course, the water. **Fig Tree Bay** is the resort's main beach, and **Konnos Bay**, a 200-metre cove towards Cape Greco, is the one for snorkelling. See [best beaches in Protaras, Pernera and Kapparis](/guides/best-beaches-in-protaras-pernera-and-kapparis).

## Good to know

- Step-free access varies along the promenade, and chapel opening isn't published. Check the section near you before you set out.
- Cape Greco is next door. See [things to do in Ayia Napa](/guides/things-to-do-in-ayia-napa) for the sea caves and walks.

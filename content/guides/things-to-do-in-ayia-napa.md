Ayia Napa is known for its beaches and its nights. But give it a morning and you'll find a much older town underneath.

## Half-day one: the old heart of town

### The monastery
Right in the centre stands the imposing **medieval monastery** that gave Ayia Napa its name, dedicated to **Our Lady of the Forests**. Step into the courtyard and the noise of the resort drops away. [Ayia Napa Monastery](/places/napa-monastery)

### The aqueduct and the square
Look out for the old **aqueduct**, remodelled by the Venetians, which brought water to the town until the nineteenth century. The town's square grew up around the monastery, and it's the natural place for a coffee before you head on.

## Half-day two: sculptures, cacti and Cape Greco

### The Sculpture Park
On **Kryou Nerou Avenue**, overlooking the sea, the Sculpture Park sets works by artists from around the world against the Mediterranean. The **cactus park** right next door has winding paths and benches along the way. It's open **from sunrise to sunset**, so the late afternoon light is the time to go.

### Cape Greco
Carry on east to **Cape Greco**, a national forest park of cliffs, sea caves and walking paths. The **Cape Greco Environmental Centre** explains the park's wildlife and why it's protected. [Cape Greco](/places/cape-greco)

**Please note:** in May 2026 the Department of Forests put temporary fencing around parts of the **sea caves** after serious accidents linked to jumping, and signs of instability in the rock. Follow the signs and don't jump. [Sea caves](/places/sea-caves)

## Good to know

- Midday in summer is hot for outdoor sights. Go early or late.
- Wear proper shoes on the Cape Greco paths. The rock is uneven.
- Heading out after dark? Ayia Napa's centre is a short walk from most hotels.

Paphos was once the capital of Cyprus, and it shows. Some of the finest Roman mosaics in the Mediterranean lie a few minutes' walk from the harbour cafés.

## Day one: mosaics and the harbour

### The Archaeological Park of Kato Paphos
Start here, by the harbour. The ancient city moved to this spot near the harbour, and its **Roman villas** still have their floor mosaics, scenes from myth laid in tiny stones. The park is part of the **UNESCO World Heritage** listing. [Kato Paphos Archaeological Park](/places/kato-paphos)

### The harbour and the castle
Walk out to **Paphos Castle** at the end of the harbour, then pick a seat for sunset. This is the classic Paphos evening. [Paphos Castle](/places/paphos-castle)

## Day two: the Tombs of the Kings

North of the harbour, the **Tombs of the Kings** are cut into solid rock. Some imitate the houses of the living, with rooms opening onto a courtyard lined with columns. Despite the name, the people buried here were nobles and high officials, not kings. [Tombs of the Kings](/places/tombs-kings)

Go early, while it's cool. There's little shade.

## Tickets and opening hours

Visit Cyprus lists admission at **€4.50** for the Archaeological Park and **€2.50** for the Tombs of the Kings. Both open **08:30 to 19:30 from 16 April to 15 September**, and **08:30 to 17:00 from 16 September to 15 April**. Holidays can change this, so check before you go.

## Accessibility

Both sites are **partly accessible** to wheelchairs. At the Tombs of the Kings, the accessible view is from above.

## Getting around

Kato Paphos is easy on foot. **Karavella** station in the town centre serves both town and Intercity buses, and Tombs of the Kings station serves the coast, including Coral Bay.
